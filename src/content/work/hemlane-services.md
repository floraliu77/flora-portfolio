## Impact

The product saw 2X weekly package upgrades in the first week of launch. The Services tab also became the home for future features: within 6 months we added one new feature (which I also designed), and it slotted into the tab quickly.

## Design process

As a fast-paced, compact startup, Hemlane moves quickly in product design and development. We worked in an Agile environment that encouraged collaboration and quick iterations. Problems were typically defined up front by the PM or stakeholders.

<figure><img src="/work/hemlane-services/design-process.webp" alt="Hemlane design process diagram" loading="lazy" /></figure>

## Background

### Users were churning for products we already had but were hard to find

In Q3 2023, the product team identified a significant issue: customers were churning to competitors because they were unaware of our existing features. The solution was a Services tab that displays and promotes product features.

## The goals

- Create a new tab to display all offered services and promote the ones a user hasn’t subscribed to
- Design individual service pages that show summarized data and link to the right places for goal-specific actions

## What I did

- Designed the all-new Services tab to feature Hemlane’s services
- Collaborated with cross-functional teams to learn about specific services like Eviction Guard and Repair Coordination
- Designed each service’s feature page, including services that had no detail page at the time

## Research

I conducted a UX audit, analyzed competitors, and gathered existing best practices.

<figure><img src="/work/hemlane-services/research.webp" alt="Competitor audit board comparing how property management tools display services, tasks and dashboards" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>Competitor audit</figcaption></figure>

## Hurdles

#### Overview vs. details

Stakeholders disagreed on whether the tab should be a hub of links or hold details and CTAs. We agreed the Services tab would focus on showing and promoting features.

#### Keeping consistency

Four features were available at the time, and there was no design library. I designed the four features one at a time, using the existing in-app styles.

## Testing & iterating

I ran multiple design sessions with the PM, cross-functional partners (including eviction specialists for Eviction+), and developers. After launch, no major revisions were needed, and I moved on to the next big project: [Bank Sync](/work/hemlane-bank-sync).

## Takeaways

### Tackling the big by going small

This was one of my biggest projects, with the most individual pages and modals. It felt intimidating at first: every feature page seemed important, and it was an entirely new tab in the product. Working with the product manager and cross-functional partners like the eviction service managers, I learned to break the project into individual feature pages, then into smaller detailed pages.

It's easy to get lost in the details, so I kept connecting back to the “why” behind each design. That said, I also enjoyed getting into the nitty-gritty: how the cards were organized, the language of each CTA, and the graphic in each card.
